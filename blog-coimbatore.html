Coimbatore & Its Rich History: From Ancient Kovanpudur to the Manchester of South India

Ilayanila Taxi Travel Desk Published 2026 7 Min Read Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu

Nestled gracefully at the foot of the misty Western Ghats along the Noyyal River, Coimbatore (affectionately known as Kovai) is Tamil Nadu’s second-largest city. Renowned nationwide for its salubrious year-round climate, industrious spirit, warm hospitality, and legendary filter coffee, Coimbatore is a harmonious blend of 1,500-year-old Chola heritage and cutting-edge industrial prowess.

Ancient Name

Originally named Kovanpudur after the tribal chieftain Kovan who settled the region centuries ago.

Manchester of South India

Pioneered South India's textile revolution with over 100 spinning mills and cotton weaving centers.

World-Famous Siruvani Water

Famed globally for Siruvani water — celebrated as one of the sweetest natural drinking waters in the world.

Gateway to Hill Stations

The prime entry point for Ooty, Valparai, Munnar, Topslip, and the sacred Velliangiri Hills.

The Ancient Origins of Kovai

The history of Coimbatore stretches back over two millennia into the Sangam Era. Originally inhabited by indigenous Irula tribes, the settlement was governed by chieftain Kovan. The region came to be known as Kovanpudur ("Kovan's New Village"), which gradually evolved into Koyamuthur and eventually the anglicized Coimbatore.

Strategically situated in the Kongu Nadu region, Coimbatore served as a vital commercial nexus. The famous Palghat Gap — a natural 30-km pass in the Western Ghats mountain range — enabled ancient merchant caravans to travel smoothly between the Malabar coast (Kerala) and the Coromandel coast (East Tamil Nadu). Ancient Roman coins unearthed near Coimbatore attest to a thriving Mediterranean trade in spices, gemstones, and fine cotton textiles dating back to 1st Century BC.

"Coimbatore's unique geographic position at the mouth of the Palghat Gap made it the beating heart of trade, connecting ancient dynasties from Rome to the Coromandel coast."

Key Epochs in Coimbatore's History

9th Century
Chola Dynasty Era

King Karikala Chola built the foundation of the famous Perur Pateeswarar Temple along the Noyyal River, paving the way for magnificent Dravidian stone architecture and irrigation tanks.

14th Century
Vijayanagara & Nayak Expansion

The Vijayanagara Empire and Madurai Nayaks brought stability, establishing fortified garrisons, water reservoirs, and fostering temple arts across the Kongu region.

1768 - 1799
Kingdom of Mysore & Tipu Sultan

Hyder Ali and his son Tipu Sultan recognized Coimbatore's military importance, expanding the Kovai Fort during the Anglo-Mysore wars before British annexation in 1799.

1888
Industrial Genesis (Textile Boom)

Sir Robert Stanes established the Coimbatore Spinning and Weaving Company, marking the dawn of Kovai as the textile capital of South India.

The historic 1,500-year-old Perur Pateeswarar Temple, built by the Chola Dynasty on the banks of the Noyyal River.

How Coimbatore Became the "Manchester of South India"

The transformation of Coimbatore from an agrarian countryside into an industrial powerhouse is a testament to the vision and hard work of its local entrepreneurs. The rich black soil surrounding the district nurtured high-quality cotton crops. In the early 20th century, the opening of the Pykara Hydroelectric Project in 1932 supplied abundant, affordable electricity, triggering an unprecedented explosion of textile mills.

Alongside textiles, Kovai earned another title: "The Pump City of Asia." Legendary innovator and polymath G.D. Naidu (frequently referred to as the "Edison of India") manufactured India’s first indigenous electric motor in Coimbatore. Today, Coimbatore produces over 50% of India's motors and pumps, alongside precision engineering parts, auto components, wet grinders, and IT technology parks.

Famous Historical & Cultural Landmarks to Visit

When visiting Coimbatore with Ilayanila Taxi, here are the iconic heritage spots you should not miss:

Perur Pateeswarar Temple

A 1500-year-old sanctuary dedicated to Lord Shiva, famed for its Kanaka Sabha (Golden Hall) featuring exquisite monolithic stone pillars with intricate sculptured figures.

Marudhamalai Murugan Temple

Perched on a scenic hillock 12 km from the city center, this ancient 12th-century temple is dedicated to Lord Murugan and surrounded by medicinal flora of the Western Ghats.

Isha Yoga & 112-ft Adiyogi Shiva

Located at the foothills of the Velliangiri Mountains, the iconic 112-foot steel statue of Adiyogi Shiva attracts spiritual seekers and travelers from across the globe.

Authentic Kongunadu Cuisine

Savor traditional delicacies such as Arisi Paruppu Sadam, Pallipalayam Chicken, Kovai Biryani, and finish with piping hot Kovai filter coffee.
